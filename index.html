From Chapter Five: Who Told You to Be Smaller?

Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie — one of the most celebrated writers alive — was told, early on, that her stories weren't "universal" enough. Her novels were full of Nigerian names, Nigerian food, Nigerian streets, and the quiet message from the gatekeepers was clear: make it more familiar, more palatable, smaller. Trim the parts that mark you as different. Shrink yourself to fit the shape the world already recognises.

She refused.

She kept the names. She kept the specificity. She wrote her world exactly as full and particular as it actually was — and that refusal to shrink is the very thing that made her work resonate across the entire planet.

The world tells you your specificity, your volume, your too-muchness is a liability to be managed. But the people who change anything are almost always the ones who refused to file themselves down to fit the room. Your fullness was never the problem. It was always the point.

About the Author

Why I wrote this book

Zita K Chukwuma

I write from lived experience about identity, rest, and the quiet work of becoming whole — including what it meant to start over alone in a new country at sixteen. Becoming Whole holds the stages I had to walk through myself: owning the mess, naming the shadow before it names you, resting without guilt, and finally letting yourself be seen. If you're standing at the edge of a new chapter and searching for a map, I hope this book meets you there.
